What this course is trying to do

You are the business co-founder for a real invention, working alongside a technical team to move it toward the world. You pick the venture’s binding constraint among five risks — technology, market, regulatory, scale, financing — and go retire it with real evidence.

Any model can produce a competent market sizing or a plausible strategy memo. What is scarce, and what this course grades, is the last mile: taste, verification, and judgment. Three things follow from that.

  1. Verification is a graded sport.Living Briefs contain seeded flaws. Catching them with evidence scores; confident false accusations cost you. Read AI output the way an editor reads a first draft.
  2. The signature standard.Every deliverable ends with a signature block. At the Board Meeting, judges pick any number on any slide and you have sixty seconds to give its provenance.
  3. AI is required, disclosed, and owned.Use frontier tools on every substantial deliverable. Attach a Judgment Log saying what the model drafted, what you changed, and what you caught it getting wrong. Presenting output you cannot defend is this course’s cardinal sin.

And: “we recommend against proceeding” is a fully legitimate, fully gradeable conclusion. Evidence that kills a path cleanly beats optimism that survives no contact with reality.
