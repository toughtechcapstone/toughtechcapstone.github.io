House rules

  • Send from your UCLA address. Submissions from non-UCLA senders are dropped automatically — that is the entire spam filter, so it has to be strict.
  • One link per submission. Send three separate emails if you have three things.
  • Context is optional but wanted. One or two sentences on why this is interesting gets published as a pull quote with your first name on it. No note is fine too — the link still runs.
  • Flag it if it’s local. Put SoCal anywhere in your note and it gets tagged as corridor news.

What makes a good submission

Not “big company announces thing.” The useful ones tend to be: a contract award or regulatory filing that reveals a strategy, a technical result that moves a figure of merit, a funding round with unusual structure, a hiring pattern that gives away a pivot, a company quietly dying. Primary sources beat coverage of primary sources.

Your note is the value-add. “This is the first time anyone has flown that chemistry” is worth more than the headline.

What happens next

  1. Your submission lands in a queue. Nothing is edited and nothing waits on approval.
  2. Friday morning, everything from that week is compiled into a numbered issue and published here.
  3. If something needs to come down — wrong link, second thoughts, anything — email and it is gone on the next build. No explanation needed.

Your submissions are attributed by first name only. Your email address is recorded so we know submissions come from the cohort, and it is never published.
