House rules

  • Send from your UCLA address. Mail from anywhere else is dropped automatically, which is how the site stays clear of spam.
  • One link per email. If you have three things, send three emails.
  • A sentence or two of context is optional, but including a note makes the link more valuable to everyone else reading it. Your note runs with your first name on it. If you are in a hurry, just send the link.
  • If the news is local, write SoCal somewhere in your note and it will be tagged as corridor news.

What makes a good submission

Anything that tells you more than the headline does. A contract award or regulatory filing that shows what a company is actually doing. A technical result that moves a number people in the field care about. A funding round with unusual terms. A hiring pattern that hints at a pivot. A company that has quietly gone under. Where you can, link to the original document rather than to an article about it.

If you know why it caught your eye, say so. A line like “this is the first time anyone has flown this chemistry” is usually more useful to your classmates than the article itself.

What happens next

  1. Your submission goes into a queue. Nothing is edited, and nothing waits for approval.
  2. On Friday morning, that week’s submissions are compiled into a numbered issue and posted here.
  3. To take something down, send an email. It will be gone the next time the site rebuilds, and you do not need to give a reason.

Submissions are credited by first name. Your email address is recorded so we know the link came from someone in the class, and it is not published.
