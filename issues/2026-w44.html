Governor Hochul Announces Phoenix Tailings to Establish $375 Million Commercial Scale Rare Earth Separation Facility in Monroe County | Empire State Development

esd.ny.gov

Phoenix Tailings is building a $375M rare earth separation plant in Rochester, creating at least 90 jobs, with full production expected in 2028. China processes about 90% of the world’s rare earths, and Phoenix is rebuilding that missing step in the US, turning mining waste and scrap into metals for magnets. It’s backed by BMW, Sumitomo and a $500M conditional Pentagon loan. The real test is running profitably at costs that compete with China.

TED leader’s $300M ‘valley of death’ fund might be just what later-stage climate tech needs | TechCrunch

techcrunch.com

Article: Tim De Chant, “TED leader’s $300M ‘valley of death’ fund might be just what later-stage climate tech needs”

Talks about the All Aboard Coalition, a roughly $300M fund backed by firms like Breakthrough Energy Ventures, Khosla, and DCVC. The fund makes equity investments to help startups raise the $100M to $200M rounds they need for first-of-a-kind plants. I felt it ties to this morning’s session on project finance and Breakthrough Energy.

This article stood out to me after today’s class discussion on the gap between early-stage equity and project finance, because All Aboard is trying to fill that “missing middle” with venture-style capital rather than loans. It made me curious how a first-of-a-kind project actually gets funded when no lender will take the technology risk, and whether a fund like this could realistically close that gap for the kinds of technologies our capstone teams are evaluating.

After today’s conversation about “Swiss army knife” business roles at early-stage startups, I wanted to share this Chief of Staff posting at Endurance Energy, a pre-revenue subsea geothermal company backed by Founders Fund, Felicis, and First Round. I found it interesting because it’s exactly the kind of broad, cross-functional role we heard about, sitting between engineering, manufacturing, and the CEO at a deep tech company heading toward its first long-term demonstration.
