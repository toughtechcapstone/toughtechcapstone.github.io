Outer Bio Emerges from Stealth with Yuna, a Platform That Keeps Ex Vivo Human Skin Alive and Measurable for Four Weeks | Outer Bio

outerbio.com

I wanted to share this article:Outer Bio Emerges from Stealth with Yuna, a Platform That Keeps Ex Vivo Human Skin Alive and Measurable for Four Weeks

What caught my attention is that Outer Bio is tackling a less obvious bottleneck in dermatology innovation: the difficulty of studying how treatments affect living human skin over time. By combining longer-lasting skin samples with AI-driven analysis, the company could make drug discovery more predictive, although its commercial value will ultimately depend on whether better laboratory data translates into better clinical outcomes.

NASA Announces Ka-Band Satellite Relay Awards for Space Communications - NASA

nasa.gov

One of the biggest hurdles for tough tech startups is securing offtake contracts, since few customers will commit before the technology is proven. NASA is retiring its aging TDRS relay satellites and turning to commercial providers instead, awarding up to $1.07B to two public companies and startup Astranis to develop Ka-band relay concepts, with options for in-orbit demos through 2029. It’s a good first step for the public sector to act as an early buyer from tough tech startups. The real test will be whether these concept awards lead to long-term service revenue.

Unlocking microbial magic: Kinkel-helmed Jord Bioscience releases first product, receives AgTech Breakthrough Award | Department of Plant Pathology

plpa.cfans.umn.edu

I chose this article because it tells the story of a University of Minnesota soil microbe becoming a commercial product, 9311, which works alongside the microbes already in the soil and beat leading industry seed treatments by an average of 2.6 bushels per acre in soybean field tests. What I found really cool is that it’s already registered in more than 25 states, backed by a library of over 6,500 soil microbial isolates, and won an industry award within a week of launching.
