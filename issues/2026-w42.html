How one controversial startup hopes to cool the planet | MIT Technology Review

technologyreview.com

Geoengineering (deliberate interventions in the Earth's climate system to counteract climate change) is attracting growing private-sector investment. Stardust Solutions recently raised $60M in a round led by LowerCarbon Capital (a climate VC with an LA presence) to develop solar geoengineering technology, raising some interesting questions around commercialization and regulation. Who is the actual customer for this technology? Governments might theoretically pay for climate cooling, but there's no established market, and the consequences of deployment are global. It's an interesting example of how, like AI, technology can advance faster than regulatory structures can keep up. Unlike AI, however, geoengineering also lacks an established commercial market. For additional context on the growing investment in this space, SRM360 published a September 2026 article titled What's New in Solar Geoengineering Funding?

— Grace

Fervo's Cape Station Geothermal Plant Goes Live - IEEE Spectrum

spectrum.ieee.org

Two articles:

On its face, it looks like Fervo Energy has crossed the valley of death. Fervo's Cape Station in Utah has become the first utility-scale enhanced geothermal plant to sell power to the grid, with a 33 MW unit now supplying Shell Energy and plans to reach 500 MW by 2028. There is still a tough road ahead, however, as Morpheus Research argues they are hiding unresolved problems: thermal decline, high water loss, induced seismicity, and selectively presented pilot data. For tough-tech commercialization, the key tension is that Fervo raised about $2 billion and signed roughly 1 GW of power contracts before proving its technology at scale, betting that costs will fall from about $7,000/kW toward $3,000/kW. The case also shows that non-technical constraints like water rights, transmission access, and investor transparency can decide whether a first-of-a-kind technology succeeds commercially.

— Brevin

SquareMind raises $18M for Swan robot with Fred Moll's backing

massdevice.com

SquareMind’s $18M raise shows how tough tech can improve clinical outcomes by combining robotics, standardized imaging, and AI with physician expertise. The goals are not to replace the clinician but reduce documentation burden and enable more consistent skin exams. Swan is a useful example of the difficult path from lab technology to healthcare impact: it must fit real clinical workflows, earn regulatory clearance, and prove that efficiency gains translate into earlier detection and better patient care.

Jeff Lawson of Inertia: Building Fusion Energy Through Factory Economics - OVERRIDE

mcj.vc

This is a podcast from MCJ, my summer internship company that focuses on clean tech, infrastructure, and industrials. Inertia ($450M Series A, led by Bessemer) approaches fusion as a manufacturing problem rather than a physics problem. Commonwealth and Helion still must demonstrate net energy production in their magnetic fusion machines. Inertia is building on laser fusion, which achieved ignition. The company is betting that it can mass-produce cheap lasers and fuel targets the way solar panels and batteries are made.
