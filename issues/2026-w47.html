Innovative 3D printed patch could improve skin cancer treatment | News | Queen's University Belfast

qub.ac.uk

I wanted to share this article: Innovative 3D printed patch could improve skin cancer treatment

I found this interesting because researchers are using 3D printing to incorporate anticancer drugs directly into dissolvable microneedles, potentially making localized treatment less invasive and more precise. What I'm curious about is whether the advantages of this delivery method can translate beyond the lab, particularly when manufacturing consistency, clinical efficacy and competition with established treatments will determine whether it becomes commercially viable.

* Gladys

— Gladys

Staera emerges from stealth with $4.6m in pre-seed funding

semiconductor-today.com

I found this interesting because Staera just raised a sizable pre-seed round to take a lab-born optical chip technology toward a commercial release still years away, which is exactly the long, staged path we talked about in class. It made me curious how a team this early decides what technical milestone to hit first so that the next round of investors takes the risk seriously.

— Michelle

Amazon Sustainability Deep Tech Accelerator: Scaling Climate Tech - US Press Center

press.aboutamazon.com

Amazon recently announced an expansion of its climate-tech accelerator, bringing together AWS, Amazon Devices, and Amazon Private Brands. The equity-free program aims to bridge the gap between technical validation and commercial adoption by connecting startups directly with enterprise decision-makers, giving easier access to potential supply agreements, licensing, and technology partnerships. Securing a commercial agreement with a company like Amazon could be a strong signal of market demand and technology viability, helping startups de-risk commercialization and attract future investment.

— Grace

Morphotonics raises €40M to expand its display tech into data centers | TechCrunch

techcrunch.com

I found Morphotonics’ €40M raise an interesting deep-tech story. The company spent 12 years to make the tiny displays inside AR glasses like the Meta Ray-Ban Display (which I am a huge fan of), and it’s now bringing that technology into data centers, where it uses light to move data between servers. There are still a lot of unknowns about data centers, so I think technology like this could make it clearer where their infrastructure is headed. It’s also telling that customers have already validated it even though no machines have shipped yet.

— Jinjae

I'd take the finer details with a grain of salt, but it is also interesting to see how some funds only invest in certain areas too.

— Zachary

Fusion startup Helion is developing new semiconductors that could double output of its power plants | TechCrunch

techcrunch.com

Sharing this TechCrunch article about Helion, a fusion energy startup that received a $175 million federal award to develop advanced semiconductors for its power plants.

What caught my attention is that reducing energy losses in one component could potentially double the power output of future fusion plants. It shows how improving supporting technologies, rather than just the core innovation, can make a major difference in cost efficiency and the commercial potential of an emerging technology.
